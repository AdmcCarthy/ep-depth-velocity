# Check shots and time-depth tables (Volve)

Code: `vsp`, `timedepth`; config `config/adapters/volve.yaml` section `vsp`; switch `vsp_checkshot` (`scripts/volve_check.py --family vsp_checkshot`); marts `well_timedepth`, `well_timedepth_summary`, `well_timedepth_survey_check`, `well_timedepth_pick_check`, `well_timedepth_pick_summary`; the analysis notebooks (`29_checkshot_time_depth`).

## 1. What is read
The small text tables of the folders `08.VSP_VELOCITY` of the log archive; the 121 SEG-Y files hold traces and are **not read**. Two layouts: three-column pairs (MD, TVD below MSL, TWT in ms) and the operator's survey table (`MD/RKB`, `TVD/RKB`, `TVD/MSL`, `TC`, Fortran D exponents; two-way time = 2000 x TC, an ASSUMPTION that agrees with the water velocity of the first row). Tables exist for 15/9-19 A, 15/9-19 B&BT2 (two wellbores in one folder), 15/9-F-11 T2 (three tables) and 15/9-F-15 A. The time reference is mean sea level as the files state it.

## 2. Checks (findings, never corrections)
- **Depth reference against the deviation survey.** The table depth is compared with the selected survey at the same MD. A table that calls its depth `MD MSL` is tested with both offsets (none, rotary-table elevation) and both residuals are kept. Result: the 15/9-F-15 A table agrees only after adding 54.9 m (median residual 1.0 m with the offset, 45.7 m without), so its depth is measured depth from the rotary table minus the air gap; the other tables agree within a few metres without an offset. This is independent support for the rotary-table elevation 54.9 m of the F wells.
- **Picks against the check shot.** The pick's depth below MSL (`-tvdss` in the picks file) is looked up inside the table and the times are compared. 15/9-F-15 A: median 0.00 ms (the picks were evidently made from this table). 15/9-F-11 T2: tables 1 and 3 agree within about 6 ms, table 2 (rig source, 50 m offset) is up to 36 ms off at the reservoir; the two shallow picks (about 830 and 1,010 m) are about 100 ms earlier than the tables that reach that depth. 15/9-19 A agrees (median 1.3 ms); the two shallow picks of 15/9-19 BT2 are about 48 ms later.
- **Which wellbore a two-wellbore folder belongs to** is decided by evidence: the table of `15_9-19 B&BT2` fits the picks of 15/9-19 BT2 (median 1.4 ms); 15/9-19 B has no pick with a time to test.
- **Resolution.** In the near-horizontal reservoir section of 15/9-F-15 A (MD 3,350-3,460 m, TVD changes by about 12 m) the 0.1 ms time resolution makes the interval velocity noise (8,000 to -13,000 m/s) and time not monotonic: 9 interval velocities fall outside 1,400-6,000 m/s (assumed band). Kept as delivered, flagged; the average velocity there is the number to use.

## 3. Limits and data to request
Four wellbore folders only; the F wells without a VSP have no tie. The picks and the check shots are not independent where the picks were made from the check shot. Shallow-pick disagreements of the older wells are unexplained (another time datum or velocity model is possible). Wanted: the seismic datum and the velocity model of the interpretation, the VSP processing reports, the seismic volumes (SEG-Y) for tie quality, and the geomodel.

## 4. Planning files not read
`Well_technical_data/StressCheck` (8 `.sck`) and `Wellcat` (3 `.wcd`) are OLE2 compound files with serialised MFC objects: a proprietary binary layout that cannot be read reliably without the vendor software. They are for planned wells (F-10 A, F-13, F-14 A, F-11 T2) and would hold the planned pore pressure and fracture profiles. **Data request:** an export of the pore pressure, fracture gradient and load cases as text or the vendor's report.

## Sonic log against the check shots: the drift (2026-11-09; `sonic_drift`, marts `well_sonic_drift`, `well_sonic_drift_summary`, `well_checkshot_table_compare`, the analysis notebooks section 4)
The composite sonic slowness (us/ft) is integrated against the vertical depth of the selected survey from the top of the sonic interval, and the check-shot time at the same measured depth (with the table's tested depth offset) is compared with it: **drift = check-shot time minus integrated sonic time**, zero at the top of the sonic by construction. A gap in the sonic up to 10 m is bridged and counted, a longer gap ends the segment, a slowness outside 40-200 us/ft is counted and kept, an isotropic medium is assumed (``config/adapters/volve.yaml`` ``sonic_drift``; the limits are assumptions). Nothing is corrected and no calibrated table is made. Only 15/9-F-11 T2 (three tables, 2579-4515 m MD) and 15/9-F-15 A (2619-3768 m MD, depth offset 54.9 m) have both records over a long interval.

Result: over 520-907 m of vertical depth the two stay within 34 ms (mean interval velocities within 6 %); the drift at the end is +25 ms (near-incidence table), -3 ms (rig-source), +33 ms (optimised) for F-11 T2 and +6 ms for F-15 A, so only 1 of 4 tables shows the negative drift (integrated sonic slower than the check shot) that the literature expects; the drift is smooth (step standard deviation 0.5-1.1 ms between levels 15 m apart). The three F-11 T2 tables differ from each other by up to 38 ms (rig-source minus near-incidence, median -5.6 ms), more than any of them differs from the sonic; the optimised table equals the rig-source table within 0.5 ms at 144 of its 249 levels and is not a simple splice of the other two. Limits: two wells; the sonic is a composite of stated but unexplained processing and an LWD sonic is not measured at the time of the VSP; the drift above the sonic top is not seen; anisotropy and borehole effects are not separated. The primer ``sonic_calibration`` (request 129) is from search summaries only.

## Sonic-calibrated time-depth table (2026-11-09; marts `well_timedepth_calibrated`, `well_timedepth_calibrated_summary`, `well_timedepth_calibrated_pick_check` and `_pick_summary`, the analysis notebooks section 5)
The calibrated table is the integrated sonic plus the drift curve (`sonic_drift.calibrated_table`): from the first check-shot level to the end of the sonic, with the drift between levels interpolated linearly (knees at the levels are not smoothed) and the last drift held below the last level (flagged `extrapolated_below_last_level`). One table is made per check-shot table (three for F-11 T2, one for F-15 A); none is called the best. It is a MODELLED relationship, not a measurement: it equals the check shot at the levels to the 1 m grid (largest difference 0.15 to 0.22 ms) and says nothing about which record is right where they differ. Columns: `md_m` (RT), `tvdss_m` (survey), `twt_ms` (calibrated, MSL), `sonic_uncorrected_twt_ms`, `drift_applied_ms`, `near_horizontal`, `time_reversal`, `interval_velocity_m_s` (over 50 m of measured depth); the assumptions (50 m window, 0.2 m per m limit for near-horizontal) are in ``config/adapters/volve.yaml`` ``sonic_drift``.

Results: tables span 2.30-2.82 s TWT over 2579-4522 m MD (F-11 T2) and 2.31-2.58 s over 2619-3782 m MD (F-15 A); drift applied -13 to +34 ms. Where the well is near-horizontal (38 % of the F-11 T2 samples, 24 % of F-15 A) time against vertical depth is ill-conditioned and the samples carry no interval velocity; in F-15 A the calibrated time runs backwards with depth at 32 samples (3417-3460 m MD). The interval velocities of the calibrated tables swing between 2.4 and 5.1 km/s over 50 m because they inherit the noise of the check-shot times at levels 15 m apart and the knees of the linear drift; use the table by vertical depth outside the flagged stretches and not as a velocity model. The picks of F-11 T2 are within a few ms of the calibrated near-incidence and optimised tables and about 26 ms from the calibrated rig-source one; the picks of F-15 A were made from its check shot, so they do not test its calibrated table. No table is made for wells without both records.
